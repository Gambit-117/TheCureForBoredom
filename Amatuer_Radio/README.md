## Amatuer Radio
Amateur Radio, also known as ham radio, is a non-commercial hobby and communication service where licensed operators use designated radio frequencies to talk across towns, states, around the world, and even into space. You must pass a government test to get an official call sign and legal permission to transmit, but once you have it you are able to communicate via voice, morse code, and digital signals freely on the bands you are licensed to operate on.

Getting certified in amateur radio is not difficult. The Amateur Radio Relay League (ARRL) is the premier National group that nearly all amateur radio operators utilize for training material, licensing, finding technology, and getting involved with the amateur radio community.

Amateur radio encompasses several technological hobbies and can serve as an excellent middle-ground for those interested in the following:
- Network Communications
- Electrical Engineering
- Cybersecurity and Digital Communications
- Embedded Systems and Hardware Development
- Emergency Communcations and Emergency Medical Services

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) is the US Government Agency that handles licensing for amateur radio operators. A break down of the licensing levels is as follows:
- Technician license: The lowest level and the first license to obtain, opening access to UHF, VHF, and HF frequencies for local and some long range communication
- General license: An upgrade from the technician license that provides more access to HF bands for longer range communication
- Amateur Extra license: An upgrade from the general license that provides nearly unrestricted access to all frequency ranges allocated to amateur radio, with the greatest expansion in allocations in the HF range

Study guides and learning material for these are available in subdirectories for each license.

Several forms of community engagement and/or challenges are available for amateur radio. Any local amateur radio club in your area can help you get involved with these. For more solo/self-guided exploration, consider the following:
- Parks on the Air (POTA): an international amateur program encouraging operation of amateur stations in public parks
- Summits on the Air (SOTA): an international amateur program encouraging operation of amateur stations from mountain peaks and hills
- Islands on the Air (IOTA): an international amateur program encouraging operation of amateur stations from islands
